Frequently asked statutory questions

How the Business Revival Ecosystem (BRE) protects buyers, sellers, and senior lenders under Indian commercial law.

1. Why execute via Section 2(42C) Slump Sale instead of purchasing OldCo shares?

In a share acquisition, the buyer inherits all historical contingent liabilities, unrecorded promoter borrowings, and pending income-tax notices. Under Section 2(42C) of the Income Tax Act, a slump sale transfers the operating undertaking (assets, customer orders, equipment) to a clean NewCo for a lump-sum price, completely isolating all OldCo legal and tax liabilities within the seller's corporate shell.

2. Is the slump sale transaction subject to 18% GST?

No. Under Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), the transfer of a business undertaking as a going concern is specifically exempt from GST. Our Business Transfer Agreement (BTA) covenants ensure the entire operational apparatus transfers intact without itemized individual asset invoicing.

3. How do we operate during the 6 to 9 months required for Factory License and Pollution Consents to novate?

We deploy a dual-stage Interim Operations Sub-Contracting Agreement. NewCo acts as principal (purchasing raw material, owning customer orders, and receiving payments), while OldCo operates the manufacturing floor on a zero-margin cost-plus job-work basis under its existing factory licenses until government novation clearances finalize.

4. What protects the senior bank/NBFC lender from the 20% Seller Note?

A standardized Intercreditor Subordination Deed gives the senior institutional lender a first ranking exclusive charge over all NewCo cash flows. The promoter's 20% seller note is fully subordinated, and automatic standstill covenants freeze seller note repayments if NewCo's rolling 3-month DSCR falls below 1.15x.
